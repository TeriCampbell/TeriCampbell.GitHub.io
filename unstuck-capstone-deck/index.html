Why this problem is worth solving now

  • Access is uneven and still inadequate.
  • Districts must prove ROI on counselor spend.
  • High school students juggle many deadlines. Overwhelm can escalate quickly once they feel behind.
  • Unstuck addresses the paralysis around where to start with one clear next action.
  • Plans include resource links and context, so students can find the right support later.
  • Adult support links can trigger account setup help or counselor check-ins when needed.

How Unstuck helps Matt move

Matt is overwhelmed with school requirements and cannot pick where to start.

  • Unstuck turns overload into a sequenced plan and one next action
  • Dashboard + toolkit keep context when he comes back
  • Check-in closes the loop; he can ask for counselor help on his terms

Big picture

  • A problem worth solving: paralysis shows up before grades slip; counselors cannot scale 1:1 at every stress spike
  • What is effective and feasible:
    • Structured intake and allowlisted resources, not open chat or RAG
    • Haiku for screening and task prefill; Sonnet for plans (affordability vs depth)
    • SAT and resource rules enforced in code, not prompt-only
    • Pilot-ready now with anonymous sessions; OAuth and email check-in deferred
  • Measuring success: first plan saved, return visits (visitor_id), check-in outcomes, resource link clicks (events), and counselor-contact signal from check-in

Ships today (student path)

  • Situation: structured intake and screened optional text
  • Tasks and Clarify: deadlines and context for the plan
  • Plan: one next action and allowlisted resources
  • Dashboard: return hub and focus task
  • Toolkit: resources tied to the plan
  • Check-in: weekly progress loop
  • Safety: distress screening on free-text fields

Ships next

  • Counselor onboarding: verified school accounts and rollout workflow
  • Parent and adult help: scoped notifications for resource setup
  • School configuration: institution-specific resources and compliance packet
  • Identity and reach: OAuth login and tokenized email check-in links
  • Institutional buyer path: counselor dashboard beyond demo aggregates (see slide 8)

Why a school would buy this at scale

  • Scale between visits: Extend structured “where to start” support across the caseload without adding a counselor FTE at every school.
  • Reporting alignment: Track resource engagement and student activity so pilot exports support grant renewals and board accountability metrics.
  • Triage earlier: Surface anonymized category and engagement patterns so counselors intervene before crises consume the calendar.
  • Pilot economics: Reduced-cost district pilot trades aggregate outcomes and usage evidence for scale pricing.
  • Buyer value: configured school context plus growing historical patterns increases switching cost.

Moat: school-specific configuration plus longitudinal aggregate data.

What is next: phased rollout plan

  • Now: immediate student utility with current live product.
    • How: Word of mouth, friend and parent networks, and counselor champions; capstone demo and portfolio drive early testers, not paid district marketing yet.
  • Next: Structured counselor-led school pilot (2–3 sites, ~6-week cohort) at reduced per-student cost to collect activation, engagement, and dashboard evidence.
    • How: Lead with counselors who want triage signal; parallel district outreach where grants or Title IV-A budgets apply. Materials: one-pager, dashboard walkthrough, pilot agreement outline, metrics readout aligned to success metrics (slide 9).
  • Then: Results-informed marketing for full-price district licenses targeting high-need demographics and caseload pressure, using pilot proof, not a cold pitch without outcome data.
    • How: Grant- and board-ready exports, per-school case studies, expand from counselor champion to district buyer with slide 8 value story.
